# Pack ch02-3 — Chapter 2: Wisdom of the Soul

Traditional: The Yoga of Knowledge · Verses 2:21–2:30 · Status: **pending**

## 2:21

**Sanskrit:** वेदाविनाशिनं नित्यं य एनमजमव्ययम् ।
कथं स पुरुषः पार्थ कं घातयति हन्ति कम्

**IAST:** vedāvināśinaṃ nityaṃ ya enamajamavyayam |
kathaṃ sa puruṣaḥ pārtha kaṃ ghātayati hanti kam

**Meaning:** Know the self as indestructible, eternal, unborn, imperishable. Then ask: how could such a person kill — or cause killing?

**Takeaway:** Understanding changes what your hands can do.

**Provenance:** sa:2:21, telang:T11, arnold:B07

**Telang [T12]:** "O son of" "cause any one to be"

**Witness [gretil]:** SANDHI-JOINT

## 2:22

**Sanskrit:** वासांसि जीर्णानि यथा विहाय नवानि गृह्णाति नरोऽपराणि ।
तथा शरीराणि विहाय जीर्णान्यन्यानि संयाति नवानि देही

**IAST:** vāsāṃsi jīrṇāni yathā vihāya navāni gṛhṇāti naro'parāṇi |
tathā śarīrāṇi vihāya jīrṇānyanyāni saṃyāti navāni dehī

**Meaning:** A person drops worn-out clothes and puts on new ones. So the self drops worn-out bodies and enters new ones.

**Takeaway:** The most famous image in the book — and it earns it.

**Provenance:** sa:2:22, telang:T11, arnold:B08

**Telang [T12]:** "casting off old clothes" "new ones"

**Consensus:** SINGLE-SOURCE — Telang covers (0.70), Arnold does not (0.40)

## 2:23

**Sanskrit:** नैनं छिन्दन्ति शस्त्राणि नैनं दहति पावकः ।
न चैनं क्लेदयन्त्यापो न शोषयति मारुतः

**IAST:** nainaṃ chindanti śastrāṇi nainaṃ dahati pāvakaḥ |
na cainaṃ kledayantyāpo na śoṣayati mārutaḥ

**Meaning:** Weapons cannot cut it, fire cannot burn it, water cannot wet it, wind cannot dry it.

**Takeaway:** List what cannot touch you. It is longer than you think.

**Provenance:** sa:2:23, telang:T11, arnold:B09-shared

**Telang [T12]:** "Weapons do not divide" "dry it up"

## 2:24

**Sanskrit:** अच्छेद्योऽयमदाह्योऽयमक्लेद्योऽशोष्य एव च ।
नित्यः सर्वगतः स्थाणुरचलोऽयं सनातनः

**IAST:** acchedyo'yamadāhyo'yamakledyo'śoṣya eva ca |
nityaḥ sarvagataḥ sthāṇuracalo'yaṃ sanātanaḥ

**Meaning:** It cannot be cut or burned. Not wetted or dried. It is eternal, everywhere, steady, firm, everlasting.

**Takeaway:** Five adjectives for what does not change; zero for what does. Notice the ratio.

**Provenance:** sa:2:24, telang:T11, arnold:B09-shared

**Telang [T12]:** "not divisible" "eternal"

**Witness [gretil]:** SANDHI-JOINT

## 2:25

**Sanskrit:** अव्यक्तोऽयमचिन्त्योऽयमविकार्योऽयमुच्यते ।
तस्मादेवं विदित्वैनं नानुशोचितुमर्हसि

**IAST:** avyakto'yamacintyo'yamavikāryo'yamucyate |
tasmādevaṃ viditvainaṃ nānuśocitumarhasi

**Meaning:** It is called unperceived, unthinkable, unchanging. Knowing the self as such, you should not grieve.

**Takeaway:** Some things are understood by admitting they cannot be pictured.

**Provenance:** sa:2:25, telang:T11, arnold:B09-shared

**Telang [T12]:** "unpcrceived" "ought not to grieve"

## 2:26

**Sanskrit:** अथ चैनं नित्यजातं नित्यं वा मन्यसे मृतम् ।
तथापि त्वं महाबाहो नैवं शोचितुमर्हसि

**IAST:** atha cainaṃ nityajātaṃ nityaṃ vā manyase mṛtam |
tathāpi tvaṃ mahābāho naivaṃ śocitumarhasi

**Meaning:** But even if you think the self is constantly born and constantly dying — even then, you should not grieve.

**Takeaway:** Krishna argues the other side too. Steelman everything.

**Provenance:** sa:2:26, telang:T11, arnold:B09-shared

**Telang [T12]:** "constantly born" "grieve thus"

## 2:27

**Sanskrit:** जातस्य हि ध्रुवो मृत्युर्ध्रुवं जन्म मृतस्य च ।
तस्मादपरिहार्येऽर्थे न त्वं शोचितुमर्हसि

**IAST:** jātasya hi dhruvo mṛtyurdhruvaṃ janma mṛtasya ca |
tasmādaparihārye'rthe na tvaṃ śocitumarhasi

**Meaning:** For to one that is born, death is certain; and to one that dies, birth is certain. So for what cannot be avoided, you should not grieve.

**Takeaway:** Grief over the inevitable is optional suffering.

**Provenance:** sa:2:27, telang:T11+T12-head, arnold:B09-shared

**Telang [T12]:** "born, death is certain" "Therefore about"
**Telang [T13]:** "unavoidable thing" "ought not to grieve"

## 2:28

**Sanskrit:** अव्यक्तादीनि भूतानि व्यक्तमध्यानि भारत ।
अव्यक्तनिधनान्येव तत्र का परिदेवना

**IAST:** avyaktādīni bhūtāni vyaktamadhyāni bhārata |
avyaktanidhanānyeva tatra kā paridevanā

**Meaning:** All beings begin unseen, appear in the middle, and end unseen. What is there to mourn in that?

**Takeaway:** The visible part was always the middle.

**Provenance:** sa:2:28, telang:T12, arnold:B09-shared

**Telang [T13]:** "source of things" "lamentation"

## 2:29

**Sanskrit:** आश्चर्यवत्पश्यति कश्चिदेनमाश्चर्यवद्वदति तथैव चान्यः ।
आश्चर्यवच्चैनमन्यः शृणोति श्रुत्वाप्येनं वेद न चैव कश्चित्

**IAST:** āścaryavatpaśyati kaścidenamāścaryavadvadati tathaiva cānyaḥ |
āścaryavaccainamanyaḥ śṛṇoti śrutvāpyenaṃ veda na caiva kaścit

**Meaning:** One person sees the self as a wonder. Another speaks of it as a wonder. A third hears of it as a wonder. And even hearing it, no one truly knows it.

**Takeaway:** The deepest things stay strange. That is fine.

**Provenance:** sa:2:29, telang:T12, arnold:B10

**Telang [T13]:** "looks" "really know it"

## 2:30

**Sanskrit:** देही नित्यमवध्योऽयं देहे सर्वस्य भारत ।
तस्मात्सर्वाणि भूतानि न त्वं शोचितुमर्हसि

**IAST:** dehī nityamavadhyo'yaṃ dehe sarvasya bhārata |
tasmātsarvāṇi bhūtāni na tvaṃ śocitumarhasi

**Meaning:** The self dwelling in every body can never be killed. So grieve for no being.

**Takeaway:** The argument closes where it opened — with grief itself.

**Provenance:** sa:2:30, telang:T12, arnold:B11-shared

**Telang [T13]:** "embodied (self)" "grieve"
